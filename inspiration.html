01

Show the drum

Four ways to represent the instrument, from a literal 3D kit to a pad grid that feels like rubber. The key idea: the screen should mirror what the hands are doing, with velocity visible.

click a drum · or play the keys
three.jsWebGLbig bet

A real kit being played

A 3D kit where sticks strike the drum you hit, heads ripple in proportion to velocity, cymbals wobble and the room light takes the pad's colour. Clicking a drum raycasts to it, so the 3D view is also an input. For the real version: load a GLTF kit (modelled in Blender or a CC0 asset), add UnrealBloomPass for glow, and let the camera slowly orbit between hits.

Refs: drumstructor 3D kit configurator · playdrumsonline (three.js + PixiJS) · UnrealBloomPass docs
SVGquick win

Top-down kit map

A drummer's-eye view: pads laid out where they sit on a real kit, with key labels. Works as a layout option next to the grid, and as a teaching aid ("the hat is under your left hand").

Ref: Codrops interactive instruments (animated SVG kit)
Canvas 2Dwave equationmedium

Pads as a drum skin

The whole pad area is one membrane. Each hit drops a wave where the pad sits, and waves from different pads interfere. Harder hits make bigger waves. A 120×90 height-field sim is cheap enough to run behind the existing grid.

CSSquick win

Rubber pads with velocity fill

An upgrade of the current pad grid: soft rubber shading, a press-down offset, an LED, and a backlight that fills from the bottom to the hit's velocity, so you can see how hard you played. Tap low on a pad for soft, high for loud.

Feel ref: hardware pad controllers (MPC-style backlit pads)
02

Full-screen moments

Visual layers that make playing feel like a show. Each pad gets its own identity (colour, shape, motion), and velocity drives size and brightness. They all stack, so performance mode is just these layers at full screen.

tap anywhere
Canvas 2Dquick win

A shape per sound

Kick floods a circle, snare sprays lines, hats tick dots across, toms wipe bars, clap throws confetti, crash flashes the room. Each sound reads instantly without labels. This is the Patatap idea, applied to a drum kit.

Ref: Patatap by Jono Brandel & Lullatone (Two.js + Web Audio)
WebGLGLSLmedium

Shader backdrop

A full-screen fragment shader with two inputs: energy (rises with each hit, decays) and colour (eases to the last pad). The kick also pushes a zoom "thump". Runs on the GPU, so it stays cheap at 4K.

Refs: Audio-reactive shaders (Codrops) · Clubber (audio → shader uniforms)
Canvas 2Dquick win

Dot-matrix hit trail

A scrolling LED-style trail of the last few seconds, coloured by pad. Calm when idle, busy when you play. A good replacement for the text "Monitor" log when you're performing rather than debugging.

Ref: Perplexity voice mode on Mobbin

Kinetic type

The pad name slams onto the screen, scaled and skewed by velocity. Huge type reads from across the room, which is useful when the laptop sits behind the pads.

Ref: Apple Music full-screen lyrics on Mobbin

Performance mode = shader + shapes + type, full screen

The three layers above stacked into one full-screen view, with the Fullscreen API (plus Screen Wake Lock in the real app so the display stays on). Tap anywhere on a phone to play, using the 8 columns as pads.

03

Play & learn

Interactivity that gives the player a goal: hit notes on time, build a loop, see your own stats. These turn the app from a sound box into something you come back to.

Canvas 2Dtiming judgebig bet

Groove highway

A rock beat scrolls toward the line. Play kick, snare, closed hat and open hat (A S D F) as notes cross it, and each hit gets judged Perfect / Good / Early / Late, with a combo counter. With real pads this becomes a practice tool: timing stats per limb, slow-down mode and a groove library.

Refs: Melodics (finger-drumming lessons with a scrolling note lane) · Memrise timing track on Mobbin · Moonrider
Canvas 2DTone.js latermedium

Loop wheel

Press record, play, and your hits get stamped onto a one-bar ring at 100 BPM (one ring per pad), then the bar loops back so you can layer on top. A circle shows a bar of music better than a strip: the downbeat is always at 12 o'clock.

Ref: Chrome Music Lab (Rhythm experiment)

Live session stats

Tempo detected from your hits (try tapping a steady beat), hit counts per pad and average velocity. Cheap to build, and it doubles as a hardware check: a pad that never registers, or always hits at 127, shows up immediately.

06

How it plugs into the app

Every input source (MIDI, serial, keyboard, mouse) already ends in playPad(pad, vel) in web/app.js. Broadcast one event there, and any visual layer can subscribe without touching the audio path.

// web/app.js: in playPad(), right after flashPad(pad, vel)
window.dispatchEvent(new CustomEvent('drumhit', {
  detail: { id: pad.id, hue: pad.hue, vel, at: performance.now() },
}));

// web/visuals.js: each layer just listens
addEventListener('drumhit', ({ detail }) => {
  bursts.spawn(detail);      // shape per sound
  shader.pulse(detail);      // energy + colour
  highway.judge(detail);     // timing feedback
});

// one shared loop, paused when hidden
function frame(now) {
  for (const layer of layers) if (layer.visible) layer.draw(now);
  requestAnimationFrame(frame);
}
  • Sound first. Visuals never sit between the hit and the sound. Audio starts in playPad as it does now. Visuals only listen.
  • Drive visuals from hits, not FFT. The hit event has the exact pad and velocity, with no added lag. Use the analyser only for ambient effects.
  • Velocity is the main parameter. Map it to size, brightness, particle count and camera shake. That's what makes soft and hard hits look different.
  • Every pad has an identity: a hue (already in PADS), a shape and a motion.
  • Photosensitivity. Cap full-screen flashes at 3 per second, honour prefers-reduced-motion (this page does), and add a "Calm visuals" toggle.
  • Protect latency. Use one rAF loop, pause what's off screen, cap devicePixelRatio at 2, and keep watching the latency pill while visuals run.

Suggested order

Ordered by impact per evening of work. The quick wins all reuse the same hit event, so the first one also sets up the rest.

Quick wins

  1. drumhit event + visuals.js with one rAF loop
  2. Velocity fill + press-down on existing pads
  3. Shape-burst canvas behind the pad grid
  4. Kinetic pad name and the dot trail in place of the monitor
  5. Session stats (tempo, counts, avg velocity)

Next
